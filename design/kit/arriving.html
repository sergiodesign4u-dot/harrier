Atom

Arriving

Loading, and it is a shape that fills. 4 instances across 4 screens, at the top of a list that is still filling.

Never a spinner and never a skeleton of the whole screen. A spinner says the product is thinking and tells the analyst nothing; a skeleton of the queue draws eighteen rows that do not exist, on a surface where a row is a decision. Two pixels of bar say the same thing and lie about nothing.

Variants

Axis a reader looks forValuesWhy the cell is empty

size–2px, and it spans whatever it is put on top of

When to use it

At the top of a list that is still filling. The order settles when the count does: rows arrive as Clerk correlates them, and the annunciator says when the queue is complete.

Where she meets it. On arrival, for under a second, three times a shift. It is the shortest lived thing in the product and it still had to be decided, because the alternative was a spinner and the alternative teaches nothing.

Rule and anti-rule

Do

Two pixels above the list that is filling. It says something is arriving and claims nothing about what or how much.

Do not

       

Never a skeleton of the rows. On a surface where every row is a decision, drawing eighteen rows that do not exist is the product inventing work for a second and then taking it away.

States

It has none, and it is not interactive.
