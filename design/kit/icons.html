Foundations

Icons

Sixteen glyphs on a 24 unit grid, written once in design/system/icons.js. Every drawing on this page is built from that file at render time, and so is every number: the boxes, the centres and the two audits below are measured off the rasterised paint when the page loads, not transcribed from anywhere.

This is also the honest page of the five foundations, for two reasons that have not changed. Thirteen of the sixteen stand on no screen at all, and the page says which three do. And the set still exists twice, because a CSS mask cannot read JavaScript: the three applied glyphs are carried a second time as data URIs in the files that use them. That copy is named at the foot of this page and it is now checked rather than trusted.

Anatomy

One glyph on the grid it is actually drawn on. The faint lines are the 24 unit modules and the stronger ones every fourth, which is also how the centre of the cell is drawn: 12 is a multiple of 4, so the grid already marks it and a second crosshair on top would read as a thicker grid. The dashed rectangle is the safe field of 2 to 22, and the amber mark is the centre of the ink.

  1. A 24 unit grid, and one square for all sixteen. The viewBox never changes, so the set is resized by the box and never by redrawing. Three sizes are in use: 24 as the grid itself, .icon at 20 beside 14px text, and .icon--sm at 16 beside 12px.
  2. A 1.5px stroke, declared in one place. HARRIER_ICON_GRID.stroke in icons.js, and every svg on this page takes the number from there as an attribute rather than restating it in CSS. The three glyphs applied to the product do not obey this, and that is the finding at the foot of the page.
  3. Square terminals: butt caps, miter joins. The product's radius is zero, so a rounded cap would be the only curve anywhere in it. The join matters more than it looks: a miter is what puts paint outside the path at a sharp corner, and it is why one of the two safe field breaches below is on a glyph whose path stays comfortably inside.
  4. currentColor, and no colour of its own. An icon inherits the severity or the accent of whatever it sits in, which is the whole point of a set on a product where colour is scarce.
  5. Paint stays inside the safe field, 2 to 22. Not the path: the paint. A stroke is centred on the contour, so half of it sits outside, and a rectangle drawn at 2 is already 0.75 over the line. The field keeps a gap between two glyphs set beside each other and stops a glyph touching the edge of a control it sits in. Measured below.
  6. The centre a glyph is balanced on is the centre of its ink. The centre of the box is arithmetic and the eye does not read it: a glyph whose weight sits low reads low even when its box is centred. The offsets are listed below rather than corrected, because several of them are the correction.

Optical balance

The box is measured by paint, not by path. Each glyph is stroked onto an offscreen canvas at 20 times scale with the set's own stroke width, butt caps and miter joins, and the box is the extent of every pixel at half coverage or more. That catches what getBBox() cannot: half the stroke standing outside the contour, and a miter standing further out again. The quantum is one twentieth of a unit, so every figure here is good to 0.05.

The solid amber rectangle on each cell below is that measured box, and the dashed one is the safe field. Six glyphs are shown, chosen by the page rather than by hand: the widest, the narrowest, the tallest, the shortest and the two nearest the median area. The full sixteen are in the table.

Audit: the centre of the ink against the centre of the cell

This is a list to review, not a verdict. The centre of the ink is the coverage weighted centroid of the painted pixels, which is the closest a computation gets to where the eye puts the weight. An offset means the glyph will sit off centre in a box that centres it arithmetically. Several of these are correct: an arrow that points up carries its head at the top and should, and a bar chart standing on a baseline is heavy at the bottom by construction. The set, all sixteen

Each on its own grid with the safe field. Thirteen of these stand on no screen. They exist so the set can be judged as a set, and so that the first icon anyone reaches for is taken from here rather than drawn again. Every icon added to the product is a decision with no wireframe behind it, which is exactly the class of thing that drifts if it is not settled once.

The product contains zero svg elements across all 62 screens, confirmed by grep and recorded in docs/inventory.md section 5. Two thirds of the content axis of the control census is therefore empty: no control anywhere carries an icon before its label, and none carries an icon alone.

Three are applied, and each one had to earn its space

Design principle 5 puts density first and the product read perfectly with none, so adding an icon had to be argued rather than assumed. These three are attached as CSS masks rather than markup, and that is not convenience: wireframes/ is frozen and every coloured page holds a structural diff of exactly zero against its grey original. An svg element on a screen would end that. A mask paints the glyph with background:currentColor, so the icon still inherits whatever it sits in.

Not applied, and the reasons are the argument rather than an omission. The four verdict actions already carry a keyboard letter, so an icon would be a third signal for one action. The six state chips are words that are already the shortest form of themselves. Severity is a count of bars and a colour and does not need a third. And an icon on a banner or an empty state would be decoration on a surface that exists to be read once and left.

One source, and the copy that cannot read it

The sixteen live in design/system/icons.js as an ordered list of primitives: a path keeps its d string, a rect its four numbers, a circle its three. They are not flattened to path data, because four of the sixteen are drawn with a rect or a circle and so are the masks, and flattening would make every comparison below report a difference in the text where there is none in the drawing.

The second copy is real and it is named here rather than hidden. A CSS mask takes a data URI and cannot read a JavaScript object, so source, expand and keyboard are written again: once in the component file that uses each one, and once more wherever a second component draws the same glyph. Nothing renders the two side by side, so a correction made to one would leave the other on the old geometry and no page would look wrong. What replaces trust is a check:

node design/kit/checks/icons.mjs

It decodes every mask data URI it finds in every component file, parses the SVG back into the same primitives, and compares them character for character. It carries no table of which mask ought to be which glyph: each one is matched against the whole set, so a mask that matches nothing is reported as an unregistered glyph rather than passing quietly. The run against the current files matches all three on geometry and finds no unregistered mask. It finds the stroke instead.

Finding: the same glyph is two weights

.icon in design/kit/kit.css and .k-icon in design/kit/_page.css both declare stroke-width: 1.5. Every mask data URI declares stroke-width="1.8". Those two rules are the only stroke widths written as CSS anywhere under design/, and the 1.8 has no comment beside it in any file that carries it and no row in DESIGN.md. The geometry agrees exactly, character for character, so this is not a drawing that drifted: it is one drawing painted 20 per cent heavier in the product than on the stand that documents it.

The box sizes are read from design/kit/kit.css: the mask sits in a square element with no-repeat center/contain, so a 24 unit stroke lands on the screen scaled by the box. The column on the right is that arithmetic, computed in this page. It is what makes the difference more than a number in a file: the keyboard glyph is a 16px box in the shell, and .icon--sm is also 16px, so the same glyph at the same size is 1.20px in the product and 1.00px on this page.

Recommendation

Bring the masks to 1.5 and check what breaks, rather than raising the set to 1.8. Three reasons, in order. The set is sixteen glyphs and the masks are three, so 1.5 is the majority and the documented value. The 1.8 makes the one safe field breach in the applied trio worse, not better: . And the likely reason for 1.8, which is that a 1.5 stroke in an 11px box lands at and goes grey on a 1x display, is a case for a hinted small size in the set rather than for a second weight nobody wrote down. Nothing here changes the product masks. This page reports; the change is a decision with its own line in docs/decisions.md.
