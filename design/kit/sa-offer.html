Standalone offer

It offers to put the case back in its list. 3 instances across 3 screens, at the top of a case opened by its own address, and it is the standalone route admitting what it is.

A case reached by permalink, or out of a chat message, or from a pager, has no queue beside it. The verdict controls all work, and what is missing is everything around them: the seventeen other cases, the row that changes when you file, the next thing to look at. One line says so and offers the way back.

It is hidden at 360, and that is the point. On a phone the standalone rendering is not a fallback, it is the design: node 3.1 settles that selecting at 360 opens the standalone case rather than a pane covering the list. Offering to open the queue there would offer the analyst the surface she deliberately does not have.

It was not in the register. The step 2 census counted controls; this is a container whose only control belongs to btn, so the census recorded the button and never the line it stands in. One of the thirteen found at step 5, in docs/inventory.md section 13.

Anatomy

Read this section at the desk. Below 900px the component removes itself, so at 360 the two halves below are empty by design and the note under them is the whole rendering.

  • .sa-offerthe line. A wrapping flex row aligned on the baseline, with --space-3 of padding under it and a hairline below that. The rule is what makes it a header for the case rather than a first paragraph of it
  • .btnthe offer, in the outline emphasis and never the filled one. It is btn unchanged: this component contributes nothing to it but a place to stand
  • .whywhat you get by taking it, at 11px. flex: 1 1 var(--space-7), so it takes whatever is left beside the button and wraps under it once 48px is no longer enough. min-width: 0 is what lets it wrap instead of pushing the row wider

When to use it

Only on the standalone case route, and only at a desk. It is not a back link and not a breadcrumb: it does not go where you came from, it goes to the same case rendered in the surface that has the list beside it. If the queue is already on screen, there is nothing to offer.

Where she meets it. When somebody has sent her a case rather than her having picked one up: a permalink in a chat, a link out of a ticket, a case quoted in a handover. She is at the desk, she has been dropped into the middle of the console, and this is the first line she reads. On the phone at 03:00 she never meets it at all, because there it would offer her the console she cannot work in.

Rule and anti-rule

Do

Open this case in the queueThe next case is there. This page stayed on the one you just ruled on because it has no list to move you into.

An outline button and one sentence naming what the other surface has that this one does not. It says what is true about the page you are on, which is the same standard the product holds Clerk to.

Do not

Open this case in the queueFor the full experience, open the case in the console.

Never the accent ground, and never a sentence that says this page is the lesser one. The offer is btn in its outline emphasis, because the filled emphasis on a case belongs to the verdict. All four verdict controls work here; the queue is offered second, not instead.

Between 900px and the row’s natural width the sentence wraps under the button rather than squeezing beside it, which is what flex-wrap and a 48px basis buy. Below 900px there is no rendering at all, so at 360 the two boxes above hold their paragraphs and no example. That is the component and not a gap in the page.

States

It has none. The button inside it carries hover, active and the focus ring, and the container adds nothing. What it does have instead of a state is a viewport at which it stops existing, and that is not a state: nothing about the case changes, the offer does.

Narrow this page to 360 and the two benches above go empty. That is the component behaving, and it is the only way to see it: display: none under 900px is a rule about the viewport, so no snapshot and no smaller box reproduces it. The rule sits in components/sa-offer.css rather than on the screens, so a fourth standalone state inherits it without anybody remembering to.

No out of reach form, and it could not have one. The queue is either the surface you are on or the surface you are not, and there is no case where opening it is refused. Where the console itself is down, the whole page is outage and this line is not on it.
