Atom

State

A word that reports a condition. 194 instances across 44 screens: the largest component in the product by count. It says what has already happened to a case, and there is nothing to press.

It was half of chip until step 2. chip--state had to turn the pointer off with cursor:default, which is the shape of a component pretending to be another one. Splitting it is why this file declares no states at all, and why chip no longer has to say default.

No affordance mark, and that is the whole difference from chip. A chip carries a caret or a cross because pressing it does something. A state carries neither because pressing it does nothing, and the absence is the signal.

When to use it

Thirteen words on three surfaces, not six.

The stage 07 kit page said "the set of six is closed", and that is true of one surface. Counted in the grey corpus, which is the whole product:

Adding a fourteenth word is a voice decision before it is a component one. Every one of these is in voice/docs/microcopy.md, and the sets are closed there. A state that is not in one of the three sets means either a new surface or a word that should have been one of the existing ones.

Rule and anti-rule

Do

decided unrecorded

Side by side in a row's states cell, reporting what is true. Neither offers to be pressed and neither needs to: what you do about them is in the pane.

Do not

All tenants ▾

Not for something that narrows a list or changes the scope. That is chip, which looks close and is a different component: it has a pointer, an affordance mark and four states, because there is something to press.

States

It has none, and it is not interactive. No hover, no active, no focus, no out of reach.

Giving it a hover would be the same defect as inventing a role: it would say the word can be pressed, and it cannot. What changes underneath it is the row, and the row is a different component: a selected row repaints its ground and the state sits on the new one without knowing.
