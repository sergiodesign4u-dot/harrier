Molecule

Consequence

What will happen if you file this. 9 instances across 9 screens, in the reject and escalate dialogs, and it says what follows rather than what you are doing.

The dialog title already says what you are doing. This box says what comes after: the case closes, or it goes to a named person, or one tenant’s baseline changes. Design principle 3 says the override is one key and it teaches, and this is the half that teaches.

min-height: 58px, and it is the only fixed height in the system. The box is filled by the choice above it, so without a floor the dialog footer moves as you pick a reason, and the button you are aiming at is the one that moves. Two lines of consequence is the common case, and the floor is two lines.

It was not in the register. Step 2 built the register from a browser census of controls, and this is a text container: it reports and is never pressed, so it never entered the list to be measured. Found at step 5 against kit.css, and docs/inventory.md section 13 carries all thirteen.

When to use it

In a dialog, once, directly under the choice that determines it, and above the footer. Never twice on one surface: two consequence boxes mean the dialog is asking two questions and should be two dialogs. If nothing downstream changes, say that in the box rather than leaving it out, which is what the tenant baseline rejection does.

Where she meets it. Every time she disagrees with Clerk, which is the moment the whole product is built for and the moment she is most likely to give up and accept instead. Reject already costs four taps against accept’s two; this box is the return on the extra two, because it is where she finds out that her reason went somewhere.

Rule and anti-rule

Do

Goes to the tenant baseline for Larkfield Logistics. Nothing outside this client changes, and the next signal like it arrives already scored.

Where it goes, what it is called when it gets there, and what does not change. The two bolded parts are the answer; the sentence after them is the reason she can hand to somebody else.

Do not

A reason is required before you can file this rejection.

Never a requirement. That is hint, which stands in the dialog footer beside the button it is about, 11 times out of its 18. Spending the only fixed height in the system on a rule the footer already enforces buys nothing, and it costs the reader the one place she looks for the consequence.

At 360 the box keeps its floor and its padding and the text reflows inside it. The floor is a min-height rather than a height for exactly this reason: three lines at 360 grow the box instead of clipping the third.

Limits

Rules of composition and context, which no anti-rule can carry: nothing else is the right component to take, and what is wrong is the count or the neighbour. Counted on the grey corpus at stage 09, and every one of them is a function in design/kit/checks/rules.mjs. Full table with sources on Architecture.

  • R8. A .only-desk element never renders at 360, and a .only-narrow never renders at the desk.

States

It has one, and no pointer produces it. .is-empty is set by whether a reason has been chosen above, so it is a state of the dialog rather than of the box. Beyond that there are none: it reports, and it is not pressed.

The two boxes are the same height, and that is the component working. Both stand at the 58px floor whatever they hold, so the footer under them does not move between the two renderings. Watch the dashed border and not the text: it is the only thing that changes, and it changes because the answer is not settled rather than because the box is empty.

No hover, no active, no focus and no out of reach. There is nothing to press, so the four interactive states have nowhere to attach. The dashed border is deliberately not a disabled treatment: disabled says you may not, and what is true here is that you have not yet.
