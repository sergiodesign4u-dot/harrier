Chip

The scope of what you are looking at, and the way to change it. 122 instances across 44 screens, every one of them in scopebar: the filters above the queue and the scope of a rejection.

Half of this component left at step 2. chip--state was 194 instances of a word that reports a condition, and a word you cannot press is not a chip. It became state, its own component, and what stayed here is only what is pressable. That is why this file declares cursor:pointer with no modifier turning it off, which kit.css had to.

Variants

Two axes. The first is how settled the filter is; the second is what pressing it will do, and it belongs to the content rather than to a class.

When to use it

Only in a scope bar, and only for something that narrows a list or names what a decision applies to. Design principle 4 says tenant context is never more than a glance away: the chips are that glance. Rasha carries 40 or more tenants and the same signal is a Tuesday at one client and an incident at another, so the bar above the queue has to say which clients she is looking at before she reads a single row.

Where she meets it. Twice a shift, and never in the middle of ruling on a case. The scope is set at the start and then left alone, which is why a chip is quiet at rest and why the caret is the only thing offering to be pressed.

Rule and anti-rule

Do

All tenants ▾Sort: default ▾

Set and unset side by side, and you can tell them apart without reading either. The dashed one is an offer; the solid boundary is something actually narrowing the list.

Do not

escalated unrecorded

Not for a word that reports a condition. Those two are state, drawn here as chips to show what the mistake looks like: they offer to be pressed and there is nothing to press. state is a different component with cursor:default, no affordance mark and no states at all, and it is written in the marks round.

out of reachdoes not existA filter that cannot be applied is not shown. The scope bar renders the filters that apply to what is on the screen, and there are zero disabled chips in either corpus

The inverted chip takes no hover, and this is a finding rather than an omission. Both of its hover surfaces are already spent: the ground is the invert plate, which is the top of the ramp with no step above it, and the boundary is what identifies it against that plate, so raising it to --line-hover would put bone on bone at 1.00. The cure is not a token for two instances, and the rule that one use is not a role says so. It is a product question: the inverted plate is what this system uses for a thing that reports, and an interactive chip probably should not wear it. It goes to backlog.md at step 6, and until then the pointer and the cross carry it.
