Molecule

Contact

Who you reach, and what they are on for. A name, its qualification and what to say, in a box of their own, because handing a case on names a recipient and you have to be able to read that name back to somebody on a call.

It is where an open question lands. CLAUDE.md leaves it unanswered whether the SOC lead uses the console at all, and says node 4.6 depends on the answer because an escalation names a recipient. This component is 4.6’s answer as currently drawn: it addresses a person read from the rota, and the rota is a system Harrier does not own. If the answer changes, this is the file that changes.

Where it actually stands is worth reading before you place one. The header of components/contact.css puts it in the escalate dialog at 6 instances across 3 screens. Counted on the frozen wireframes, the class itself wears 3 renderings across 3 screens, all three of them on the outage page, where it names the provider’s duty line. The other three of the six are out-contact, which docs/inventory.md assigns to outage as a zone. The escalate dialog is still grey and carries its recipient under a class of its own.

When to use it

Once on a surface, for one person or one staffed line, when the next move is off the console and onto a call. Never as a directory: two of these side by side turn a decision into a choice, and the point of the box is that it has already been made. Where the console is up and the handoff is inside the product, the recipient belongs to the escalate dialog and not to a free-standing box.

Where she meets it. Rarely, and always badly: the console is not answering and she still owes the client an answer. That is the argument for the 17px name. She is reading it off a screen she does not trust, to a phone, at whatever hour the outage happened, and the one thing that must survive that is the name.

Do not

soc-escalations@provider.example

The shared mailbox for out of hours.

Never a system. A mailbox, a ticket queue or a rota tool is a string you paste, and the component for a string you paste is addr, which gives it a ground and a copy button. This box is for a recipient who answers, and dressing an address as one implies somebody is on the other end.

At 360 nothing about the box changes and nothing needs to: it is already a single column, and the name is the only thing above 14px. This is the one surface in the product where the phone rendering is the important one, and the layout was chosen so there would be no second rendering to keep true.

States

No interaction states, and one domain state. It is read and quoted, never pressed, so there is no hover, no active and no focus ring, and no link inside it either: the number is a value the provider declares and Harrier does not carry one. What it does have is .is-gone, which is a fact about the world rather than a response to a pointer.

Two roles carry it and neither is a new one. The strike and the dim are --text-secondary, the ink the meta line already uses, so the name drops to the level of its own qualification instead of disappearing. The dashed boundary is the same --rule-edge at a different style, which is why the pair in the two themes is automatic and this state needed no token of its own.

It was owed from stage 08 and could not be placed until now. The census read the whole grey corpus and still could not count this state: the four screens that draw it are the escalate family, all four of them grey, so the only recipient it could see was the outage page’s duty line, which is never gone.
