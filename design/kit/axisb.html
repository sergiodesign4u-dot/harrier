Second axis

Where a rejection goes, and the reason four taps never became five. 3 instances across 3 screens, in the reject dialog, directly under the reason you picked.

CLAUDE.md measures the cost: rejecting Clerk takes four taps against accepting’s two, and design principle 3 says the override is one key. What stage 04 bought is that five of the six reasons derive the second axis. When the reason settles where the case goes, this component shows the answer locked rather than asking again. Only the sixth reason opens it as a list.

The locked form is dashed and not disabled. It is not a control that cannot be used: it is an answer that has already been given, by the choice above it. A disabled control here would be the fifth tap dressed as a sixth, and the fifth tap is the one stage 07 was told it may not add.

It was not in the register. The step 2 census counted controls, and in its dominant form this holds none: the locked box is text in a box. Found at step 5 by scanning kit.css against the register, one of thirteen, all listed in docs/inventory.md section 13.

When to use it

In the reject dialog, once, under the reason list, in the block headed Where it goes. Which form it takes is not a design choice made per screen: it is decided by the reason above it, and the taxonomy decides that. If you find yourself choosing between locked and open, the pairing has not been settled and that is the thing to settle.

Where she meets it. In the seconds after she has decided Clerk is wrong, when the interface is still costing her taps and accepting is still two. She sees the locked form far more often than the open one, and every time she sees it the product has spent a keystroke she would otherwise have paid.

Rule and anti-rule

Do

Held, not routednowhere yet, and counted

The reason at full strength, the destination beside it, and a dashed border saying the answer came from the choice above rather than from you. Even the answer nowhere is stated, because a rejection that goes nowhere and a rejection nobody decided about are different records.

Do not

The tenant baseline

Never a disabled control. [aria-disabled="true"] on a btn or an opt says you may not do this; what is true here is that you already have. Where the axis is genuinely open the answer is opt in a list, offered and pressable, and where it is settled the dashed box says so without pretending to be a control at all.

At 360 the locked row wraps inside its own border: both children are flex items with an automatic minimum size, so the destination drops under the reason rather than pushing the box past the dialog. Nothing is hidden, because the reason and the destination are the two halves of one sentence.

States

It has none. Locked, there is nothing to press: it reports an answer. Open, every state belongs to opt, which brings its own hover, active, focus ring and chosen ground, and the container adds nothing on top of them.

Point at the locked box

nothing, in either theme. No ground, no border change, no cursor.

Point at an option

hover, from opt. The whole row takes a ground and the answer is one number key away.

Press Tab

the ring lands on the two options and skips the locked box, because the locked box is not focusable.

Put the two forms side by side and the argument is visible in one look. The same destination, the tenant baseline, appears in both: once as an answer you are being told and once as an answer you are being asked for. Solid borders and a pressable ground on the second, a dashed border and no response on the first. Nothing about the locked form says unavailable, and that is deliberate.

What it reads, and where it stands

--text-secondaryink 4.5:1the locked box, its destination, and its dashed border, which is the one place in the system an ink role draws a line
--text-primaryink 4.5:1the reason that settled the answer

Two roles and no line role at all. The dashed border reads --text-secondary rather than --line-control, and that is the drawing being consistent with itself: the border and the text inside it are one greyed statement, so they move together when either theme changes. It also means the border is measured as ink and comfortably above the 3:1 a control boundary would need.
