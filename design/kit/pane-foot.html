Organism

Pane foot

This is where the analyst rules. Accept, Amend, Reject, Escalate: four buttons that carry the main job of the whole product between them, and 86 of the product’s 157 buttons stand here. 35 instances across 35 screens.

It is sticky at the bottom of the pane, so the decision is reachable without scrolling back from the end of nine signals. margin-top:auto pushes it down when the case is short, position:sticky holds it when the case is long, and a ruling that has to be scrolled to is a ruling that gets postponed.

Sticky and never stuck, twice over, and both were found by measurement. At the desk, min-height:100vh on the shell let it grow with its content, so there was no scroll container for bottom:0 to resolve against; the shell is pinned to exactly one viewport now and the columns scroll instead of the page. At 360 the shell stops scrolling and the document takes over, but the pane kept its own overflow-y:auto, so bottom:0 pinned to a box that never scrolls and the decision sat two viewports down. Node 4.2 had found that and fixed it privately for one screen; every other pane kept the defect until stage 07.

Anatomy

What the bench is standing in. A 340px box at --pane-w with the pane’s ground and its own overflow-y, which is z5. bottom:0 resolves against the nearest scrolling ancestor and nothing else, so a bench that did not scroll would show a foot standing still and prove the opposite of the component. Scroll inside it: the evidence moves, the four buttons do not.

  • .pane-footthe strip. A wrapping flex row with two steps of gap, three of padding down and four across, and an edge above it
  • margin-top:autoit sits at the bottom of the pane even when the case is two paragraphs long, rather than floating under the last line
  • position:sticky; bottom:0the declaration the component exists for. It resolves against z5, and only while z5 is the thing that scrolls
  • background:inheritthe foot takes the pane’s own surface, whichever it is. A paper pane and a console pane both get a foot that matches, and neither needs a variant
  • .btnone to four of them. The first is --primary and carries the accepted verdict; the rest are outline, and each carries its key

When to use it

At the bottom of a detail pane that is asking for a decision, and only there. The route decides how many buttons: four on a case, two on the permalink, one on a shift that has been sealed, none on a record.

Where she meets it. Dozens of times a shift, at the end of every case she reads. This is the strip that carries the main job in the whole product, and design principle 3 is measured against it: accept costs two taps and reject costs four, and the fourth is Enter rather than a confirmation. The fifth is what nothing after this stage may add.
