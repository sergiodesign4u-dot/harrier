Atom

Claim tag

Two or three words that say what kind of claim follows, and then get out of the way. 44 instances across 22 screens, inside claim, which is the line of evidence in the case narrative.

Design principle 2 says the cheapest correct thing first and depth one key away. The tag is the cheapest thing on the densest surface in the product: it lets the analyst skip a claim she has already understood without reading the sentence that carries it.

Variants

None. The words vary and the component does not.

One literal closed here. kit.css wrote letter-spacing: .06em, which is exactly --track-mono, measured on plate J and read by no product rule until now. Same computed value, one fewer way to say it, and the typography page is down to seven unresolved tracking values from eight.

When to use it

At the head of a claim in the case narrative, and nowhere else. It is not a label and not a category: it is the first two words of the sentence, set in a different voice so the eye can use them as an index.

Where she meets it. Reading Clerk's evidence, on the surface the whole product exists for. Six hours a day means she reads hundreds of these, and the tag is what lets her read the third claim without reading the second.
