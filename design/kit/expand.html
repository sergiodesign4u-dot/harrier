Molecule

Expansion

The depth, one key away. 27 instances across 23 screens, every one of them inside a block. Of the 27, 21 have a head and close, and 6 do not.

Design principle 2 ends here. Cheapest correct thing first, depth one key away: the expansion IS the depth. The claim reads without it, the working is inside it, and the analyst who trusts the verdict never opens one. More explanation is not more trust, and past a point it reduces it.

  • summarythe line you press. It stays visible when the expansion is closed and it says what is inside rather than saying more. It is also the switch between the two fillings: a filling with a head closes, a filling without one never had anything to close
  • the left rail2px in --line-separator, so the open expansion reads as an aside on the claim rather than as a new section
  • summary::beforethe mark, one of the three icons this product applies. It earned its space because depth had no mark on it at all. It hangs off the head and not off the box, and that is what keeps the two fillings honest: a filling with no head loses the mark by construction rather than by a second class somebody has to remember. As a flex item of the line it labels it carries no offsets at all, and it holds wherever the type scale goes next. It used to be an absolutely positioned pseudo of the container, 11px to the left of the border and 9px above the optical centre: two unmeasured offsets that together put the glyph in open space with nothing beside it, reading as an orphan rather than as a mark on anything

The five summary elements on the door are NOT absorbed by this component, and that plan is dropped here rather than left standing. The rename map at step 2 read them as the same job under a different name. They are not: the door’s help is a question on a full page with one form on it, and this is depth on a claim inside a list of evidence. Giving the door a bordered surface with a left rail would say there is working to check where there is only an answer to a question. doorhelp keeps its own summary and its own file.

When to use it

Under a claim, when there is working worth showing and not worth showing first. All 27 are exactly that. The door’s help looks like one and is not: see the note under Variants.

Where she meets it. On the cases she does not believe. That is the measure of the component: opening one is a signal that Clerk has not made its case, and a product where every expansion gets opened has a verdict problem rather than a disclosure problem.

Rule and anti-rule

Do

how Clerk counted thisThree sessions in the six hours before the case opened.

A summary that names what is inside, and the working under it. It opens in place, under the claim it belongs to.

Do not

Read more

Never a button and never a dialog. A button says a decision is being taken; a dialog covers the evidence she is deciding on. Both break the one rule this component exists to keep.

Limits

Rules of composition and context, which no anti-rule can carry: nothing else is the right component to take, and what is wrong is the count or the neighbour. Every one of them is a function in design/kit/checks/rules.mjs. Full table with sources on Architecture.

  • R13. An expansion that has a head is a details, and no head holds a link. A head written on a div renders as an unstyled summary with a UA triangle and nothing behind it, which is exactly what the chevron on the old div promised and could not do. A link in a head is unreachable, because the click belongs to the disclosure.
  • R3. No overlay covers the detail pane. This component is the one most likely to have been drawn as one.

States

Open and closed are the element’s own state and need no class. Press the summary. Closed is how every one of the 21 arrives on the screen, and that is the whole point of the component: the analyst who trusts the verdict never opens one.

Motion

MomentWorkDurationCurveUnder reduce
the mark turns as the expansion opensresponse--dur-fast--ease-standard1ms through the token, and the expansion still opens

One moment, and it is the cheapest of the three works. The glyph is drawn pointing down, so open is its rest and closed is the rotation: what turns is an acknowledgement that the key landed, not an arrival. Nothing else here moves. The body does not slide, because a height that animates is a height the browser recomputes every frame, and this component stands 21 times in a list the analyst reads at speed. What prefers-reduced-motion drops is the movement and never the state.
