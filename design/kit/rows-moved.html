Moved rows

The list of what moved while you were away. 5 instances across 5 screens, inside a block in the shift brief, and nowhere else.

The container the row split also created, and it is the reason the organisms went from 16 to 19 at step 2 rather than to 18. When .rows--moved .row became its own component, the list around it became one too: it holds a different row, it does not scroll, and it draws a rule above itself because it sits inside a block rather than filling a zone.

It does not scroll, and that is the whole difference from rows. A handover is read from the top in one pass. A scroll box here would hide the last thing that moved behind a scrollbar, inside a document that already scrolls.

Anatomy

What the bench is standing in. A 240px scrolling box, which is brief: the surface that scrolls in the product. Scroll it, and the list goes past with the document rather than inside a scrollbar of its own. That is the component, and a bench that gave the list its own height would have hidden it.

Step 2 ruled the rename on the anatomy criterion and step 6 executed it: the five screens carry rows-moved now, and the row inside them carries row-moved. Not one pixel moved. The reconciliation table in docs/inventory.md is where both rows are recorded.

When to use it

In the shift brief, in the block that answers what moved while you were away, once. It is not a general list container: anything that fills a zone and can run past its own height is rows.

Where she meets it. Twice a shift, at the two moments the console is not about a case: coming on, and handing off. That is the second of the three MVP jobs, and this list is the part of it that answers what did I miss. Everything in it has already happened, which is why nothing in the line is a decision and why there is no severity column to sort by.

The head row takes no states either, and it is a variant rather than a component. Same argument as the queue’s head: it names columns, it is not a decision, and it cannot be opened. 5 of the 35 handover lines in the product are heads.

At 360 there is no shift brief at all, so there is no narrow rendering of this list to document. Z4 hides the brief below 900, because coming on shift happens at a desk with two monitors and the phone has one scenario, a paged case read and escalated. The bench above keeps the desk rendering at that width and scrolls sideways to hold it, which is the nearest honest thing to showing nothing.

The container itself declares no media query. The line inside it keeps its four zones wherever it stands, because row-moved reads --row-tracks-moved directly rather than through the :root collapse that flattens the queue row to one column.
