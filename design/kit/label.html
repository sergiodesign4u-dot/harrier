Label

The name of an answer. 31 instances across 15 screens: above a field 30, and once in an option list.

It is a name, never a heading. Why you are rejecting, On Larkfield Logistics, C-4417, Email, Your wording. Uppercase mono at 11px because voice/docs/voice.md gives a machine-readable name a different register from the prose around it, exactly as tag and state do, and secondary ink because the answer is the thing being read and this is only its name.

A reason is required

  • .labelthe whole component. A real label element with a for, on all 31
  • fornot a style decision, and it cannot be traded. It is what makes the field's name reach a screen reader, and what makes the name a click target that focuses the field. A div drawn to look like this would lose both and nothing on the screen would say so

The tracking is .08em, the most used of the six mono values in the product, and it is a literal rather than --track-mono at .06em. The typography page carries the open question about all of them, and nothing was collapsed here to make a token look used.

When to use it

Above a field, always, and above nothing else. It is not optional: a box with no name is a box people guess at, and 21 of the 34 fields in this product ask the one question the analyst will be quoted on months later.

Where she meets it. Only when she disagrees with Clerk. Accept touches no field and no label; reject touches both, which is why the label's job is to be read once, quickly, and never again.

Rule and anti-rule

Do

Above the field, with a for, in the words the question is actually asked in. Clicking it focuses the field, which the browser does for free once the element is right.

Do not

Not inside the field as a placeholder. It disappears the moment she starts typing, which is exactly when she needs to check what was asked.

States

It has none, and it is not interactive.
