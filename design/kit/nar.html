Narration

What Clerk found, in order. 151 instances across 37 screens, inside a block, in the case narrative.

Design principle 2 says Clerk shows its work up to a ceiling, and this is the work. The claims and their sources sit under it and the depth is one key away, because more explanation is not more trust: past a point it reduces it.

A slot has no variant matrix, and that is the finding rather than a thin component. Six molecules turned out to be slots at step 2, measured in a browser: between them they show 71 distinct sets of child zones and one computed form each. Their pages say so, so nobody looks for a matrix that does not exist.

When to use it

In the evidence block of a case, and in a log entry showing what was decided. It is Clerk’s account of an investigation that already happened, in the order it happened.

Where she meets it. Immediately after the verdict. She reads the verdict, then reads down this list until she either believes it or does not, and the time column is what lets her stop early.

Rule and anti-rule

Do

04:14Sign in from a new ASN, 3 of 3 sessions.

A time, what happened, and the count with its scope. The line reads as a sentence and the column of times reads down.

Do not

Sign in from a new ASN, most sessions.

Never without the time and never with a vague quantity. voice.md requires a number to name its claim, its scope and its window, and a narration without a time is not evidence, it is a summary.

States

It has none, and it is not interactive.
