Readout

The one sentence that sums the list. 45 instances across 45 screens, always directly under the scope bar.

voice/docs/voice.md requires a number to name its claim, its scope and its window, and to lead with a count and never a bare percentage. This is the product’s shortest instance of that rule: two counts and their scope, in one sentence, above two hundred rows.

When to use it

Under the scope bar on every queue. It is the only sentence between the filters and the rows, and it exists so the analyst knows the size of the shift before she starts it.

Where she meets it. First, every time she opens the queue. It is read in under a second and never read again, which is exactly what one line of 12px with two bold numbers is for.

Rule and anti-rule

Do

18 waiting across 12 of 40 tenants

Count, scope, denominator. She can act on it without opening anything and she can check it against the list below.

Do not

30% of tenants have cases waiting

Never a bare percentage. 30% of forty and 30% of four are the same string and not the same shift, and voice.md forbids it in as many words.

Limits

Rules of composition and context, which no anti-rule can carry: nothing else is the right component to take, and what is wrong is the count or the neighbour. Counted on the grey corpus at stage 09, and every one of them is a function in design/kit/checks/rules.mjs. Full table with sources on Architecture.

  • R11. One h1 per screen. Closed in colour at stage 12: node design/kit/checks/rules.mjs design prints 0 broken. Still open in grey on twenty one screens, where at 360 there is no h1 at all, because there the heading is the queue readout and the list column is not rendered. It stays open because wireframes/ is frozen and not because a coloured screen is wrong.
  • R6. One readout per screen. It is the counted claim about what you are looking at, and a second would be a second claim.
