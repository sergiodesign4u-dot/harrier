Molecule

Latitude ladder

This is the differentiator, and it is a component. 26 instances across 26 screens: for one tenant, every action Clerk could take, marked yes or no, with the ceiling shown and the reason almost always where the answer is no. Almost, since stage 13: `unearned` carries a reason on a yes, because latitude granted with no record behind it is the row that most deserves her attention.

CLAUDE.md names calibrated trust as the only surviving differentiator and requires stage 04 to carry it with a concrete element on the reference screen. This ladder is that element.

It was not in the register. Step 2 built the register from a browser census of controls, and this is a container. Found at step 5 by scanning every class that owns a rule block in kit.css against the register: twenty one came back and thirteen were components. docs/inventory.md section 13 has all of them. This is the one that matters most of the thirteen: mark was written and the ladder it lives in was not, and mark’s own page says the three readings on the screens belong to the ladder row.

When to use it

In the case pane, for the tenant the case belongs to, and on the fleet where forty of them are the resting state of the right pane. It answers the third of the three MVP jobs: know where the agent’s record has earned latitude.

Where she meets it. While deciding whether Clerk should have acted rather than asked. Out of reach is shown and not hidden, and 101 of the 153 marks are `no`: hiding them would make the ladder a list of what the agent CAN do, and she needs the shape of what it cannot.

Rule and anti-rule

Do

yesContain a single endpoint
noDisable an accountabove latitude here

The ceiling given a ground, and everything above it receded with its reason. Two rows and she knows how much rope this agent has on this client.

Do not

yesContain a single endpoint

Never only the permitted actions. A ladder that lists what the agent can do is a capability list; what makes it calibrated trust is that it also shows where the agent stops.

At 360 on the standalone route the reason column is dropped: the answer is the column and the explanation is a line the analyst does not have room for. That rule lives with z5, which is the route that changes it.

States

No interaction states, and two states of the record. Nothing in the ladder is pressed, so there is no hover, no active and no focus ring. What it does have is two rows that may not read as routine, and both are facts about the tenant rather than responses to a pointer.

CLAUDE.md binds this about a case and it is true one rung up: a thing that changed must not look identical to a thing that did not. Without the class this reason was byte for byte the four under it, and the ladder was saying nothing had moved on the one screen whose whole subject is that something did. The row keeps .off, so the mark and the action stay exactly where they were; what moves is the reason, from --text-secondary at 400 to --text-primary at 600. The same rule, and a different half of the declaration does the work. A ceiling is already --text-primary at 600, so ink and weight are inert on it and the COLUMN is what moves: a ceiling never carried a reason, so it was never given the reason track. With the class the reason lands right aligned in track 3 at --size-xs; without it, it starts at --size-sm in the same track, ragged against every other reason in the ladder and hard against the action name it qualifies. It stands once in the product, on tenant-no-record.

Node 3.5 section 7 ordered this one. It calls a ceiling with too few rulings behind it the state that most deserves the analyst’s attention, and requires the cell shown AND marked. Without the class the ladder read the same as a tenant with thirty six rulings behind the same grant, which is the reading that hides the risk rather than the reading that shows it.

Neither state spends a colour, and neither spends a boundary. The accent has four jobs and no fifth, and this ladder already refused it once for the ceiling. A border on one row of a subgrid comes out of THAT row’s tracks alone, so the three columns stop lining up, which is the defect the rest of this component exists to record.
