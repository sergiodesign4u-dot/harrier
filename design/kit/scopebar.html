Scope bar

What am I looking at, answered before the first row is read. 38 instances across 38 screens: exactly one per surface that has one.

Design principle 4 says tenant context is never more than a glance away, and the analyst carries 40 or more tenants: the same signal is a Tuesday at one client and an incident at another. The bar is that glance, and it is the reason the queue can be a flat list rather than forty grouped ones.

Behaviour at width

Below 1280, the system's one point (Width), the padding drops from --space-3 --space-4 to --space-2 --space-3. That is the whole rule. The bar wraps at every width because it is a flex row with flex-wrap and nothing in it is counted: three chips or four is a fact about the screen, and neither the count nor the window changes the bar.

When to use it

Above a list that can be narrowed: the queue on 38 screens, and the reject dialog where the chips name what the rejection applies to.

Where she meets it. Twice a shift, and never in the middle of ruling on a case. The scope is set at the start and then left alone, which is why the bar is quiet at rest and why the caret is the only thing offering to be pressed.

Rule and anti-rule

Do

All tenants ▾

Set and unset side by side, above the list they narrow, with a hairline separating them from it.

Do not

escalatedunrecorded

Never states. A bar that promises to change the list and holds words you cannot press is the one mistake this component invites, and state has its own page saying the same thing from the other side.

States

It has none, and it is not interactive. The chips inside it are, and they carry their own.
