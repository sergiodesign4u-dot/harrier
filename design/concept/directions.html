Stage 06 · Concept, step 4

Three layout directions

One brand, three compositions. The palette, the type and the square corner were settled on plate J at step 3 and are not reopened here: every value on this page comes through a variable from design/_theme.css, so the three directions differ in where things sit and how much room each is given, and in nothing else. Each is drawn at a real 1440 and scaled to this column, so the proportions you are reading are the proportions on the desk.

The screen is 3.1 Case Queue, the reference screen, taken from the top row of wireframes/docs/screens.md and not re-derived. The content is the wireframe's own: the same seven rows, the same fixtures, the same fleet at rest in the pane.

The empty space under the last row is the wireframe's, not a composition defect. The queue shows the same seven cases the wireframe shows and says 7 of 18 shown beneath them, so rows eight to eighteen would fill it. It is left rather than padded out with invented cases, because a fixture that exists only to make a drawing look finished is the thing ia/docs/pages/reading-conventions.md forbids.

One thing all three obey, and it is not a preference. CLAUDE.md settles that the main dashboard is the queue with the fleet in the pane, and that the fleet has no navigation item of its own because it is the pane at rest. A fourth direction that moved the fleet out of the pane would be reopening that decision rather than composing a screen, so it is not on this page.

A · Ledger

The bet

Scanning is the primary act, so the screen is optimised for how many decisions are visible at once. The split is 62 to 38, the scope band gets its own row, the readout gets its own line, and every case is one line of seven columns.

What it buys

The verdict wins its row on typography, which is design principle 1 made visible rather than asserted. above latitude here becomes a mono flag beside the verdict instead of a second line of prose. The pane is 58 per cent wide, which is where the case file will actually be read.

What it costs

The same seven cases now need two lines each, so a taller row buys the emphasis. The tenant drops to mono at 10px beside four other mono facts, and tenant context is never more than a glance away is principle 4, so this is the direction most at risk of failing it.

C · Wide bench

The bet

The split does not have to be vertical. The queue takes the full 1440 so no column is ever squeezed, and the pane becomes a band across the foot. The fleet at rest is then seven tenants side by side rather than a list, which is closer to reading a fleet than scrolling one.

What it buys

Every column gets its full comfortable width and the verdict column gains 50px over direction A. The fleet reads as a fleet: seven ceilings side by side, differences visible without moving the eye down a list. This is the composition plate J itself uses.

What it costs

The case file, when one is open, gets 232px of height and the full width, which is a wide short document rather than a tall one. Evidence lines, the verdict, the provenance and four actions have to fit that band or the analyst scrolls, and no overlay may hide the evidence forbids solving it with a sheet.

What this step is waiting on

The recommendation is C, and the reason is that it is the only one of the three that makes the differentiator legible without a second action. Seven ceilings side by side, read across rather than down, is what the fleet of earned trust, made readable where the analyst works was supposed to mean. A and B both leave latitude as a list that has to be traversed.

The honest objection to C is in its own cost column and it is not small. A case file 1440 wide and 232 tall is a shape nobody has drawn yet, and the whole of stage 04 was drawn against a tall pane. If the verdict, the evidence lines, the provenance and four actions do not fit that band, C fails, and it fails at step 6 rather than here. A is the direction that cannot fail, because it is what the wireframe already proved, and its cost is only that nothing in the row wins on typography.

Step 5 builds the stand concept.html from whichever of these is chosen. Step 6 colours the reference screen for real and proves it in a browser at 1440 and at 360.
