Stage 06 · Concept, step 5

The visual language, standing up

Everything the brand decided, alive rather than described: the palette on the ground it lives on, the two families at the sizes the interface actually uses, the square corner drawn 1:1, the closed set of state chips, the four verdict actions, and the reference screen in the composition chosen at step 4. Every value on this page comes through a variable from design/kit/kit.css, so if a value is wrong here it is wrong in one place, and one edit moves it everywhere.

Where each value came from, with its origin line, is DESIGN-artifacts.md at the root. Why this brand and not the eight that were not taken is in the same file. Why dark is the ground, decided against the reading research, is in docs/decisions.md.

1. The reference screen

3.1 Case Queue in direction A, Ledger. The composition is the wireframe's own, chosen at step 4 against a recommendation for direction C. Drawn at a real 1440 and scaled to this column.

That is design/queue.html itself, running in this page, not a drawing of it. The stand used to hold a hand built replica of the screen, and the stage 06 audit found six components where the replica and the product had drifted apart: different chip family and size, different button geometry, different row density, different fleet row tracks, different bar heights, and a solid chip that was on the accent here and not on the real screen. A replica of a screen that exists is a second copy, so it was removed rather than corrected.

Open against this screen, and step 6 owes an answer

Nothing in the row wins on typography. Design principle 1 says every row is a decision and the verdict is the decision, but in direction A the verdict is set at the same size as the tenant and the description. Direction B spent a second line on solving it. Step 6 has to solve it inside one line, with weight, colour or position, or B's argument comes back as a finding rather than a preference.

2. Palette

Twelve values. Six are named roles on the plate, three are surfaces measured inside the console it drew, and three are the severity ramp, which is derived rather than sampled and says so. Taken as pixels and then checked against the plate's own stylesheet, which agreed at every role. Full sample points in DESIGN-artifacts.md, section 2.

The accent is spent, not applied, and the list was counted in a browser rather than asserted. On the finished screen it does four jobs: latitude, on the fleet and on the case; the live state; where you are in the navigation; and the row being decided together with its primary action. The stand said three, which was true of a poster and false of a screen, so the number is corrected here rather than defended. One thing was taken off the list on sight: the solid unrecorded chip had picked up the accent, and it is loud enough inverted, so spending the accent on it would have made the accent mean less everywhere else.

A2 was amended at step 5 and the amendment is written down rather than absorbed. Severity now reads as colour rather than as a count of bars, which means saturation is spent in two places instead of one. A2 changed from saturation is scarce to saturation is spent on two closed sets and nothing else. It is still countable: three things on the accent and three levels of severity, and a fourth use of colour anywhere is a defect. concept.md section 5 named this exact conflict before it happened and left the decision to the user, which is where it was made.

4. Geometry

Radius on the interface is zero. Measured on the plate rather than read off a caption: every radius the plate draws is on a physical object, and no chip, button, panel, row or stage box carries one. This overrules the 2px that references.md carried in from Axiom, and it meets attribute A1 more strictly than the reference did.

Depth comes from tone, never from blur. Attribute A4. Three grounds separate the whole console: the board, the panel, and the row being ruled on. There is no shadow and no blur anywhere in the interface, and the only shadows in the whole brand are on the physical objects of the plate.

8. Proved on two more screens

Roles are not named here. They only become visible after components have stood on real screens, which is stage 08, and inventing them now means renaming them twice and breaking the origin line on the first rename.

The language is proved on two more screens, and the proof is what they cost. A language is not one good screen. It is proved when a screen that shares little with the first still reads as the same product, and the honest measure of that is how much new CSS the second screen needed.

  • Case queue, the reference. Nine variable assignments plus the short list of what the brand adds.
  • Case file, the contrast. The queue compressed into a narrower column, so the same row stands at a different density, plus a whole prose surface: what Clerk filed, what happened, nine evidence lines with their sources, and the four verdict actions. One new rule, because the primary action inverts to the text colour by default and had to take the accent instead.
  • Shift brief, the harder test. It shares almost nothing with the queue: no severity, no bars, no fleet rows, a different block system and a handover table. Zero new rules.

All three carry a zero structural diff against their grey originals, asserted byte for byte after every edit, so what changed is provably the visual layer and nothing else. All three were checked in a browser at 1440 and at 360. Pairs are in design/concept/screens/.

What is still not here. No spacing scale, no semantic token names, no component anatomy, no light theme. Those are stages 07 and 08.
